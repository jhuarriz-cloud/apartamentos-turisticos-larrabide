Where to Stay in Pamplona on the Camino de Santiago

Every year, thousands of pilgrims arrive in Pamplona after crossing the spectacular Pyrenees from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port through Roncesvalles. For many, this is the first major city on the Camino de Santiago (French Way) and one of the most memorable stops of the entire pilgrimage.

After one of the most demanding stages of the Camino, choosing the right accommodation can make all the difference. A peaceful night's sleep, modern facilities and a comfortable place to recover will help you enjoy the following stages with renewed energy.

Whether you are walking the Camino or travelling by bicycle, Pamplona offers the perfect balance between history, culture, gastronomy and relaxation.

Why stay overnight in Pamplona?

Pamplona is much more than a place to sleep.

It is the historic capital of Navarra, internationally known for the San Fermín Festival but equally appreciated by pilgrims for its welcoming atmosphere, medieval streets and excellent services.

Many walkers and cyclists decide to spend one or even two nights here after completing the mountain stages across the Pyrenees.

A rest day allows you to:

  • Recover after one of the hardest stages of the Camino.
  • Explore the medieval Old Town.
  • Visit the Cathedral of Santa María.
  • Walk through Plaza del Castillo.
  • Discover the impressive city walls.
  • Enjoy traditional Navarrese cuisine.
  • Wash your clothes.
  • Prepare your backpack or bicycle.
  • Continue towards Puente la Reina feeling refreshed.

For many pilgrims, Pamplona becomes one of the highlights of the French Way.

What can you do in Pamplona in one afternoon?

Even if you only have a few hours before continuing your pilgrimage, Pamplona has plenty to offer.

The compact historic centre is easy to explore on foot. Narrow medieval streets lead to lively squares, historic churches and excellent restaurants serving traditional pintxos and local wines.

Some of the most popular places to visit include:

  • Plaza del Castillo
  • Cathedral of Santa María
  • The ancient city walls
  • Portal de Francia, where the Camino enters the city
  • Ciudadela Park
  • Calle Estafeta
  • Plaza Consistorial

Many pilgrims are surprised by how relaxed and welcoming Pamplona feels compared with larger Spanish cities.
